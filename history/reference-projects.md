# Great projects reference

**Type:** reference

Lookup sheet for the great projects in the history lessons, for an engineer who needs a date, a size, or the lesson a project teaches. Each row gives one number the lesson states and keeps the lesson's hedges on dates and counts.

| Project | Built | Where | Scale | Engineering lesson it teaches | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Great Pyramid of Giza | About 2560 BCE | Giza, Egypt | About 146 m tall when new, with more than two million blocks | Quarrying, surveying, transport, food supply, and accounts for thousands of workers take planning at scale | [Ancient monuments and waterworks](projects/01-ancient-monuments-and-waterworks.md) |
| Roman aqueducts and the Pantheon | From 312 BCE; Pantheon about 125 CE | Rome | Eleven aqueducts by the early third century CE; Pantheon dome about 43 m across | A controlled gentle slope moves water by gravity, and cleaning crews and rules against illegal taps are part of the system | [Ancient monuments and waterworks](projects/01-ancient-monuments-and-waterworks.md) |
| Great Wall of China | 221 BCE to 1644 CE | Northern China | Many thousands of kilometres across all periods | Walls built in phases from local earth, stone, and brick, with watchtowers for signals; forced labor was a heavy cost | [Ancient monuments and waterworks](projects/01-ancient-monuments-and-waterworks.md) |
| Grand Canal of China | About 609 CE | Eastern and northern China | About 1,800 km on the main route | Pound locks let a canal climb between water levels, and labor drafts can break the builder | [Ancient monuments and waterworks](projects/01-ancient-monuments-and-waterworks.md) |
| Qanats of Iran | From the 1st millennium BCE | Iran and surrounding dry regions | In use for more than two thousand years | A sloping tunnel keeps groundwater underground where little evaporates, but deep pumping can lower the water table until it stops | [Ancient monuments and waterworks](projects/01-ancient-monuments-and-waterworks.md) |
| Angkor water system | About the 9th to 12th centuries | Cambodia | West Baray about 8 km long and about 2 km wide | Reservoirs, canals, and sluices need constant maintenance, and when water control broke down, fields and cities suffered | [Ancient monuments and waterworks](projects/01-ancient-monuments-and-waterworks.md) |
| Erie Canal | 1817 to 1825 | New York, United States | About 584 km with 83 locks | A flight of locks and aqueducts cut freight from about \$100 to about \$10 a ton | [Canals and railways](projects/02-canals-and-railways.md) |
| Suez Canal | 1859 to 1869 | Egypt | About 193 km, with no locks | A sea-level channel avoids locks, but forced labor and disease took a disputed toll | [Canals and railways](projects/02-canals-and-railways.md) |
| Panama Canal | 1881 to 1914 | Panama | About 82 km, with locks lifting ships about 26 m | Sanitation and mosquito control decided whether the project finished | [Canals and railways](projects/02-canals-and-railways.md) |
| First Transcontinental Railroad | 1863 to 1869 | United States | About 3,000 km | Two crews built toward each other, and the line led to standard time zones in 1883 | [Canals and railways](projects/02-canals-and-railways.md) |
| Trans-Siberian Railway | 1891 to 1916 | Russian Empire | About 9,300 km from Moscow to Vladivostok | A single track limited capacity, and harsh climate and remote supply lines made work hard | [Canals and railways](projects/02-canals-and-railways.md) |
| Brooklyn Bridge | 1869 to 1883 | New York City, United States | Main span about 486 m | Steel wire cables carry a very long span, and caisson work needs protection against decompression sickness | [Bridges, dams, and towers](projects/03-bridges-dams-and-towers.md) |
| Eiffel Tower | 1887 to 1889 | Paris, France | About 300 m when it opened, about 330 m today, with about 18,000 parts | Wrought iron lattice with curved legs resists wind and reaches great height | [Bridges, dams, and towers](projects/03-bridges-dams-and-towers.md) |
| Forth Bridge | Opened 4 March 1890 | Scotland | Main spans about 521 m | A steel cantilever spans long distances and needs constant maintenance against corrosion | [Bridges, dams, and towers](projects/03-bridges-dams-and-towers.md) |
| Hoover Dam | 1931 to 1936 | Nevada and Arizona, United States | About 221 m high, with about 2.5 million cubic metres of concrete | Pouring concrete in blocks cooled by pipes keeps curing heat from cracking the mass | [Bridges, dams, and towers](projects/03-bridges-dams-and-towers.md) |
| Golden Gate Bridge | Opened 27 May 1937 | San Francisco, United States | Main span about 1,280 m | A deep stiffening truss resists wind and traffic, and a safety net under the deck caught falling workers | [Bridges, dams, and towers](projects/03-bridges-dams-and-towers.md) |
| Three Gorges Dam | 1994 to 2006 | Yangtze River, China | About 185 m high, with about 22,500 MW of generators | Large projects carry social costs, such as relocating more than one million people | [Bridges, dams, and towers](projects/03-bridges-dams-and-towers.md) |
| Akashi Kaikyo Bridge | Opened 5 April 1998 | Japan | Main span about 1,991 m | Designing for strong winds and earthquakes sets the span, and the Kobe earthquake added about a metre to it | [Bridges, dams, and towers](projects/03-bridges-dams-and-towers.md) |
| Burj Khalifa | Opened January 2010 | Dubai, United Arab Emirates | About 828 m tall with 163 floors | A Y-shaped plan reduces wind forces on a very tall tower | [Bridges, dams, and towers](projects/03-bridges-dams-and-towers.md) |
| Radar and the MIT Radiation Laboratory | 1940 to 1945 | Birmingham, England; Cambridge, Massachusetts | Magnetron at about 3 GHz, about 10 cm wavelength; laboratory staff of several thousand | Centimetre waves give narrow beams and small antennas, and radar can be jammed and fooled | [Wartime and science megaprojects](projects/04-wartime-and-science-megaprojects.md) |
| Manhattan Project | 1942 to 1945 | Oak Ridge, Hanford, and Los Alamos, United States | About 130,000 people at peak, costing about 2 billion dollars | Thousands of specialists worked on one deadline, and criticality accidents showed the need for margins | [Wartime and science megaprojects](projects/04-wartime-and-science-megaprojects.md) |
| Apollo program | 1961 to 1972 | Florida, Houston, and the Moon | Saturn V about 110 m tall with about 35 MN of thrust; about 25 billion dollars | Lunar orbit rendezvous cut the mass to send, and the Apollo 1 fire and Apollo 13 rupture led to design changes | [Wartime and science megaprojects](projects/04-wartime-and-science-megaprojects.md) |
| International Space Station | 1998 to 2011 | Low Earth orbit | About 420 tonnes at about 400 km altitude; over 100 billion dollars | Partners shared the cost, and debris and the Columbia loss show the standing risks | [Wartime and science megaprojects](projects/04-wartime-and-science-megaprojects.md) |
| Large Hadron Collider | 2008 to present | Geneva, Switzerland | About 27 km circumference; magnets at about 1.9 K | A failed joint in 2008 led to a protection system that detects quench events and dumps stored energy | [Wartime and science megaprojects](projects/04-wartime-and-science-megaprojects.md) |
| Transatlantic telegraph cable | 1858 to 1866 | Ireland to Newfoundland | Route of about 3200 km | Signal delay grows with the square of cable length, and too high a voltage broke down the 1858 insulation | [Networks that connect the world](projects/05-networks-that-connect-the-world.md) |
| Electric grid | 1882 to 1896 | New York City; Niagara Falls | A single grid can carry many gigawatts across thousands of kilometres | Transformers let AC travel far, and the 2003 blackout showed one fault can spread | [Networks that connect the world](projects/05-networks-that-connect-the-world.md) |
| U.S. Interstate Highway System | 1956 onward | United States | About 48,000 miles, roughly 77,000 km | Standards for grades, sight distance, and interchanges suit heavy trucks, but routes displaced residents | [Networks that connect the world](projects/05-networks-that-connect-the-world.md) |
| ARPANET and the internet | 1969 to 1983 | United States | First four nodes with links at 50 kbit/s | Packet switching and open protocols became the base of the internet, and weak early security let the Morris worm spread | [Networks that connect the world](projects/05-networks-that-connect-the-world.md) |
| GPS | 1978 to 1995 | Global, from orbit | Nominal 24 satellites at about 20,200 km altitude | A receiver needs at least four satellites to solve for position and clock error | [Networks that connect the world](projects/05-networks-that-connect-the-world.md) |
| Undersea fiber-optic cables | 1988 to present | Atlantic, Pacific, and global routes | More than 1 million km of cable worldwide | Total internal reflection and erbium-doped amplifiers made long spans practical, but anchors and fishing gear cut cables | [Networks that connect the world](projects/05-networks-that-connect-the-world.md) |
