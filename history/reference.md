# History of engineering reference

**Type:** reference

Lookup sheet for the main events, eras, and ideas in the history lessons, for an engineer who needs a date, a place, or the lesson that covers it. Every row comes from a statement in the lessons, and dates marked "about" keep that hedge.

## Principles

- A simple machine saves force and costs distance, and friction changes the answer most in screws and long chains of stages, so measure the efficiency of the real parts.
- Gravity moves water over long distances when the slope is controlled, and maintenance is part of that design.
- Standard threads, gauges, and tolerances let separate shops build parts that fit, and a written standard lets any supplier who meets it make the part.
- Test the idea against measurement before committing to it, as Galileo, the Wrights, and Faraday did.
- Material choice limits what a machine can do, since iron allowed the first engines and cheap steel allowed railways and large bridges.
- Safety rules come from failures, so design in redundancy, containment, and tests that catch faults on the ground.
- Physical limits set the budget, so check them first, including the Carnot limit, line losses that grow with the square of the current, the rocket equation, and the Shannon limit.
- Tools print results that look exact, so write the units and uncertainty on every number, check a simulation against a test, and treat any growth trend as a fit to data.

## Eras at a glance

| Era | Dates | Key energy source | Key materials | Signature machines |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Simple machines](01-simple-machines.md) | Inventions run from about 3500 BCE to the first century CE | Effort applied to a machine, which adds no energy | Rope, as in pulleys and winches | Lever, wheel and axle, pulley, inclined plane, wedge, screw, shaduf |
| [Ancient engineering](02-ancient-engineering.md) | About 3500 BCE to 1500 CE | Gravity (flowing water) and organized labor | Mudbrick, stone, baked brick, lime and volcanic ash concrete, wrought iron, grass rope | Sledges and ramps, qanats, aqueducts, Pantheon dome, Inca rope bridge |
| [Medieval and renaissance engineering](03-medieval-and-renaissance.md) | About 500 to 1600 | Flowing water, wind, and falling weights | Timber, stone, brick, iron ties, metal type | Water wheel, windmill, mechanical clock, Gothic cathedral, printing press |
| [Scientific revolution and the measurement of time and place](04-scientific-revolution.md) | About 1600 to 1700 | Falling weights and pendulums | Brass rods, later invar, a bimetallic strip in H4 | Pendulum clock, balance spring watch, telescope, marine chronometer |
| [The industrial revolution](05-industrial-revolution.md) | About 1760 to 1840 | Steam from coal | Coke-smelted iron, cast iron, steel from 1856 | Newcomen and Watt engines, screw-cutting lathe, Rocket locomotive |
| [The electrical age](06-electrical-age.md) | About 1800 to 1900 | Electric current from batteries and generators, including hydroelectric power at Niagara Falls | Zinc and silver or copper discs, carbon filament, copper wire | Voltaic pile, telegraph, Pearl Street Station, AC transformer system, induction motor, radio |
| [Flight, cars, and mass production](07-flight-cars-and-mass-production.md) | About 1876 to about 1914 | Gasoline and diesel fuel | Interchangeable parts, standard threads | Four-stroke engine, Motorwagen, diesel engine, Wright Flyer, Model T on a moving line |
| [War, nuclear, and space](08-war-nuclear-and-space.md) | About 1935 to 1969 | Nuclear fission, liquid rocket fuel, and fuel burned in gas turbines | Electromechanical relays and thousands of electrical parts | Radar, jet engine, Chicago Pile-1, V-2, R-7, Saturn V, Z3, ENIAC |
| [Semiconductors and computing](09-semiconductors-and-computing.md) | 1904 to the early 1970s | Electric power for tubes, then low-power solid-state devices | Glass vacuum tubes, silicon, germanium, phosphorus and boron doping | Vacuum tube, transistor, integrated circuit, microprocessor |
| [The modern era](10-the-modern-era.md) | About 1970 onward | Batteries and solar cells | Silicon, lithium-ion cells, liquid resin | Sketchpad and finite element analysis, ARPANET and the internet, GPS, stereolithography printers |
